# 07 — The Feed Lexicon (Lane B vocabulary and delivery)

> Companion to `docs/06-feed-universe.md`. That file holds the cast, the format and the
> claims-safe engine; this one holds **the words and how they are said**.
>
> Source: measured analysis of the **full 60-minute** reference sample, transcribed locally
> (9,062 words). Rates are per 1,000 words across the whole hour.
>
> **Revised 2026-08-31 after analysing the remaining 40 minutes.** The first pass used only
> the opening 20 minutes and got two things wrong, both corrected below:
> `matter of fact` and `don't even` were concentrated in a single sketch in the first three
> minutes and are **not** signatures of the voice (see §3), and `yeah` was badly
> under-weighted — it is one of the two most frequent markers in the hour (see §5).
> Everything else converged: the core register is stable across both halves.
>
> **What this is.** A catalogue of general AAVE and internet-vernacular usage — dialect
> features, address terms, escalation markers, rhythm devices. This vocabulary belongs to a
> speech community, not to any creator, and is recorded here as a register spec. What we do
> **not** reproduce is a specific person's scripts or vocal identity: we build an original
> voice occupying this register (see §6 of `06-feed-universe.md`).

## 1. Terms of address — the load-bearing words

| Term | Rate (full hour) | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **`y'all`** | **8.4** | Plural *and* singular-aggressive. Sentence-initial: *"All right, y'all look when we get in here…"* Also *"all of y'all"* |
| **`girl`** | **7.9** | Peer-conspiracy marker. Often a complete reaction on its own. *"That's what I'm saying like girl."* |
| **`baby`** | 2.2 | Signals the mic drop. *"…and a website and baby, I'm finished."* |
| **`mama`** | 1.2 | Character-specific — 2.0 in the first 20 min, 0.8 after. Belongs to the family sketches, not to the voice |
| **`ma'am`** | 0.7 | The compliance response — *"Yes, ma'am."* |
| **`honey`** | 0.3 | Condescending-affectionate. *"Come on, honey. Let's go."* |

`y'all` and `girl` are the two stable load-bearing words — they *rose* in the back forty
(7.2→9.0 and 6.8→8.5), so they are the safest to lean on.

The register is **relentlessly second-person**. It talks *to* someone. It never narrates.

## 2. Openers

`all right` **13** · `look` **11** · `wait` / `wait a minute` **11** · `anyways` 2 ·
`okay so` 1 · `hey` 1 · `now` 2

- **`look`** — the escalation trigger. Precedes a demand.
- **`wait` / `wait a minute`** — the *reversal* trigger. It is how a bit turns on itself.
- **`anyways`** — hard topic cut, used to end a bit and start the next.

## 3. The escalation ladder

⚠ **Corrected.** The first pass over-weighted this vocabulary. Across the full hour
`matter of fact` is **0.3/1k** and `don't even` **1.0/1k** — and in the back forty minutes
they are **0.0 and 0.2**. Both were concentrated in one sketch in the opening three minutes.

**The device is real and central. The specific words are not its signature.** Use the
*structure* — a demand escalated one rung past reason at a time — and let the connective
tissue vary. Writing "matter of fact" into every script would reproduce a tic that the
source itself only used once.

The ladder from that sketch, rung by rung, is still the best worked example on file:

> don't touch nothing → don't ask for nothing → don't even look at nothing → I don't even
> want y'all smelling nothing → *matter of fact* close your damn eyes → close **both** of
> your damn eyes → don't even breathe → *matter of fact* y'all don't got arms no more →
> what is an arm, tell me what an arm is → actually stop using your legs → don't even come
> near me, I don't know you, you don't know me

Eleven rungs. Note the funniest rung — *"y'all don't got arms no more"* — carries **no
profanity**. That is the proof the device survives our constraints.

## 4. Dialect grammar

| Feature | Rate (full hour) | Stability | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ain't` | **4.1** | stable (3.6 → 4.3) | *"We ain't but three centimeters"* |
| `gon` / `gonna` | **3.5** | very stable (3.6 → 3.5) | *"how we gonna know"* |
| Negative concord | **1.7** | 2.3 → 1.3 | *"don't y'all touch nothing"* |
| Copula deletion | **1.5** | stable (1.3 → 1.7) | *"she coming"* · *"she bout to go to work"* |
| `is you` / `was you` | **0.8** | stable (0.7 → 0.8) | *"Why the hell is you breathe?"* |
| `don't got` | 0.4 | | *"y'all don't got arms no more"* |
| `bout to` (= about to) | 0.2 | first-half only | *"she bout to waste her money"* |
| `finna` | 0.0 | **never appears** | — do not add it; it is not in this speaker's register |

The four to actually lean on are **`ain't`, `gon/gonna`, copula deletion and `is you`** —
they hold their rate across both halves, which is what makes them the voice rather than a
sketch.

**Density is moderate and consistent** — this is naturalistic speech, not a dialect being
performed. Match that level. Overshooting is the fastest route to caricature, and it is the
single most likely way this lane goes wrong.

## 5. Reactions — the two biggest markers in the whole hour

| Marker | Rate (full hour) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **`oh`** | **10.0** | The most frequent marker. Often tripled: *"Oh, oh, oh"* |
| **`yeah`** | **10.4** | ⚠ **Corrected.** 5.2 in the first 20 min, **13.0** after — the single biggest drift in the sample. Effectively tied with `oh` for most-used |
| `oh my gosh/god` | 1.3 | |
| `what?` | 1.0 | |
| `no, no, no` | — | Appears as **"no no no no"** 8× in the back forty — quadrupled, not doubled |

`yeah` does more work than any single word here. It is agreement, sarcasm, a beat of
silence, and a scene transition depending on delivery. The first-pass analysis missed this
because the opening sketches are monologue-heavy while the later ones are dialogue.

## 6. Intensifiers

`so` **15** · `real` (adverbial) **13** · `whole` 2

`real` used adverbially — *"they know each other real well"* — is a signature. Not "really".

## 7. Rhythm — immediate self-repetition

A core device, and easy to miss. Repeats are not filler; **the repeat escalates**:

- **"no no no no"** — 8× in the back forty. Quadrupled, not doubled
- *"all right all right"* (4x)
- *"Jerome Williams Jerome Williams Jerome Williams"* (4x) — name as chant
- *"I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I'm sorry"* (5x) · *"let me show you"* (4x)
- *"it's right there it's right there"* · *"Oh, oh, oh"*
- **"close your damn eyes, close *both* of your damn eyes"** ← the repeat adds a word and
  raises the stakes. This is the pattern to copy.

The full hour strengthened this section: repetition is one of the most reliable devices in
the sample, and it runs to **four** repeats, not two. It also costs nothing under our
constraints — which makes it a direct substitute for the profanity we are dropping.

## 8. Sentence shape

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Words per minute (over speech) | **~197** (205 first 20 min, 192 after; conversational ≈ 140–160) |
| Median sentence | **8 words** |
| Sentences ≤ 5 words | **39%** |
| Questions | 1 in 5 sentences |

Short, fast, almost no subordinate clauses. Energy comes from **rate and brevity**, not
volume — which matters for us, because rate survives pitch-shifting intact.

## 9. Format precedent — personified ingredients already exist here

Two repeated phrases in the sample show the reference creator **already personifies
ingredients and organs**:

> *"Archer methyl xanthine, but you can call us caffeine."*
> *"If I didn't pump blood to you, you wouldn't be able to…"*

Caffeine as a character; an organ arguing with its owner. **SAFAA's cast is not a departure
from this format — it is a thing the format already does.** Strong support for the Lane B
design, and a reason to be confident the animated-ingredient premise reads natively to this
audience rather than as a brand's idea of the format.

## 10. What does NOT carry over

**Confirmed and slightly worse across the full hour: 17.4 / 1,000 words** — 15.9 in the
first 20 minutes, **18.2** in the remaining 40. That is one roughly every 57 words, about
**one every 17 seconds** at this pace, alongside racially-charged and sexual premises.
*"Shut the hell up"* alone recurs 4× in the back forty.

This was the one finding I most wanted the full hour to test, and it did not soften.

None of it ships. See `06-feed-universe.md` §6 for the reasoning and for the four devices
that carry the comedy without it. The short version: **lengthen the escalation ladder,
use the escalating repeat, go flat-deadpan against a screaming counterpart, and reach for
unexpected specificity** — *"three centimetres"* is funnier than *"short"*, and it is clean.
