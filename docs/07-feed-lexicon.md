# 07 — The Feed Lexicon (Lane B vocabulary and delivery)

> Companion to `docs/06-feed-universe.md`. That file holds the cast, the format and the
> claims-safe engine; this one holds **the words and how they are said**.
>
> Source: measured analysis of a 20-minute reference sample, transcribed locally
> (3,074 words, 219 segments). Rates are per 1,000 words.
>
> **What this is.** A catalogue of general AAVE and internet-vernacular usage — dialect
> features, address terms, escalation markers, rhythm devices. This vocabulary belongs to a
> speech community, not to any creator, and is recorded here as a register spec. What we do
> **not** reproduce is a specific person's scripts or vocal identity: we build an original
> voice occupying this register (see §6 of `06-feed-universe.md`).

---

## 1. Terms of address — the load-bearing words

| Term | Rate | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **`y'all`** | 7.2 | Plural *and* singular-aggressive. Sentence-initial: *"All right, y'all look when we get in here…"* |
| **`girl`** | 6.8 | Peer-conspiracy marker. Often a complete reaction on its own. *"That's what I'm saying like girl."* |
| **`baby`** | 2.3 | Signals the mic drop. *"…and a website and baby, I'm finished."* |
| **`mama`** | 2.0 | Character-specific |
| **`ma'am`** | 0.7 | The compliance response — *"Yes, ma'am."* |
| **`honey`** | 0.3 | Condescending-affectionate. *"Come on, honey. Let's go."* |

The register is **relentlessly second-person**. It talks *to* someone. It never narrates.

## 2. Openers

`all right` **13** · `look` **11** · `wait` / `wait a minute` **11** · `anyways` 2 ·
`okay so` 1 · `hey` 1 · `now` 2

- **`look`** — the escalation trigger. Precedes a demand.
- **`wait` / `wait a minute`** — the *reversal* trigger. It is how a bit turns on itself.
- **`anyways`** — hard topic cut, used to end a bit and start the next.

## 3. The escalation ladder

`even` **11** · `don't even` **8** · `matter of fact` **3** · `actually` **2** ·
`I don't even want` 2

**`matter of fact` never introduces a fact. It introduces a worse demand.** The ladder from
the reference, rung by rung:

> don't touch nothing → don't ask for nothing → don't even look at nothing → I don't even
> want y'all smelling nothing → *matter of fact* close your damn eyes → close **both** of
> your damn eyes → don't even breathe → *matter of fact* y'all don't got arms no more →
> what is an arm, tell me what an arm is → actually stop using your legs → don't even come
> near me, I don't know you, you don't know me

Eleven rungs. Note the funniest rung — *"y'all don't got arms no more"* — carries **no
profanity**. That is the proof the device survives our constraints.

## 4. Dialect grammar

| Feature | Rate | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `ain't` | 3.6 | *"We ain't but three centimeters"* |
| `gon` / `gonna` | 3.6 | *"how we gonna know"* |
| Negative concord | 2.3 | *"don't y'all touch nothing"* · *"Don't ask for nothing"* |
| Copula deletion | 1.3 | *"she coming"* · *"she bout to go to work"* |
| `bout to` (= about to) | 0.7 | *"she bout to waste her money"* |
| `is you` / `was you` | 0.7 | *"Why the hell is you breathe?"* · *"Is you asleep yet"* |
| `don't got` | 0.7 | *"y'all don't got arms no more"* |
| `ain't but` | 0.3 | *"We ain't but three centimeters"* |

**Density is moderate and consistent** — this is naturalistic speech, not a dialect being
performed. Match that level. Overshooting is the fastest route to caricature, and it is the
single most likely way this lane goes wrong.

## 5. Reactions

`oh` **33** (by far the most frequent single marker — often tripled: *"Oh, oh, oh"*) ·
`yeah` **16** · `oh my gosh/god` 4 · `what?` 3 · `mm-hmm` 2 · `no, no, no` · `huh`

## 6. Intensifiers

`so` **15** · `real` (adverbial) **13** · `whole` 2

`real` used adverbially — *"they know each other real well"* — is a signature. Not "really".

## 7. Rhythm — immediate self-repetition

A core device, and easy to miss. Repeats are not filler; **the repeat escalates**:

- *"all right all right"* (4x)
- *"Jerome Williams Jerome Williams Jerome Williams"* (4x) — name as chant
- *"it's right there it's right there"*
- *"no, no, no"* · *"Oh, oh, oh"*
- *"I'm so sorry… I'm so sorry… I'm so sorry to wake you"*
- **"close your damn eyes, close *both* of your damn eyes"** ← the repeat adds a word and
  raises the stakes. This is the pattern to copy.

## 8. Sentence shape

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Words per minute (over speech) | **205** (conversational ≈ 140–160) |
| Median sentence | **8 words** |
| Sentences ≤ 5 words | **39%** |
| Questions | 1 in 5 sentences |

Short, fast, almost no subordinate clauses. Energy comes from **rate and brevity**, not
volume — which matters for us, because rate survives pitch-shifting intact.

## 9. Format precedent — personified ingredients already exist here

Two repeated phrases in the sample show the reference creator **already personifies
ingredients and organs**:

> *"Archer methyl xanthine, but you can call us caffeine."*
> *"If I didn't pump blood to you, you wouldn't be able to…"*

Caffeine as a character; an organ arguing with its owner. **SAFAA's cast is not a departure
from this format — it is a thing the format already does.** Strong support for the Lane B
design, and a reason to be confident the animated-ingredient premise reads natively to this
audience rather than as a brand's idea of the format.

## 10. What does NOT carry over

Profanity runs at **16.6 / 1,000 words** — one roughly every 60 words, about **one every 17
seconds** at this pace (`fuck*` 4.6 · `damn` 2.6 · `bitch` 2.6 · `shit` 2.3 · `ass` 2.0 ·
`hell` 2.0), alongside racially-charged and sexual premises.

None of it ships. See `06-feed-universe.md` §6 for the reasoning and for the four devices
that carry the comedy without it. The short version: **lengthen the escalation ladder,
use the escalating repeat, go flat-deadpan against a screaming counterpart, and reach for
unexpected specificity** — *"three centimetres"* is funnier than *"short"*, and it is clean.
