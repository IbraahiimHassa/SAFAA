## 1. Why this lane exists

SAFAA runs two content registers on purpose (see `higgsfield-kit/projects/safaa.md`):

- **Lane A — The House.** Site, packshots, COA, packaging, investor and supplier material.
  Calm, evidence-forward, *proof as product*.
- **Lane B — The Feed.** This file. TikTok + Instagram, 9:16, comedic, character-led.

The lanes differ in **tone only**. Claims discipline, halal integrity, allergen disclosure
and AI-generation honesty are identical in both. Comedy buys no exemption. A regulator
reading a reel in isolation applies the same Regulation as to a product page.

## 2. The format

Hyper-fast animated sitcom: snap-cuts, crude-but-expressive 2D, rapid-fire visual
punchlines, pitch-shifted character voices, sound effects on every beat (whoosh, pop, slap,
mirror ding). 90-second and 30-second cuts.

**Describe the style by its attributes, not by naming a studio.** "Hyper-fast snap-cut 2D
sitcom animation, crude expressive vector shapes, pitch-shifted voices" gives more
controllable generations than naming a creator, and avoids building a commercial brand
identity on someone else's named style.

## 3. The cast — objects, never humans

**No human figures. No faces, eyes, or mouths on anything.** The characters are the actual
product packaging and raw ingredients. This is a deliberate choice and it pays three ways:
it puts the real pack in front of the viewer as the meme, it sidesteps face generation
entirely, and it keeps the lane clear of the caricature risk that a performed human
character would carry.

Emotion comes from **motion, environment and effects** — never from drawn features.

### The antagonists — **rewritten**

The original brief cast Bloating, Brain Fog and Skin Crinkle as the villains. **Those three
are retired.** A scene where the product physically defeats a bodily symptom is a health
claim in cartoon form, and every one of those three symptoms sits on the forbidden list in
`approved-claims.md` §1.

The replacements keep the comedy and move the conflict from *the body* to *the category*:

| Villain | Object | What it is | Why it's safe |
|---|---|---|---|
| **THE BASIC LATTE** | Oversized plastic cup, sweating, over-decorated | Brags about being aesthetic and trendy. 30g of sugar, all vibes, no substance | Sugar content is factual and on-label; "basic" is a taste judgement |
| **THE BEIGE SCOOP** | Chalky grey-beige tub, clumping, dusty | Undrinkable. Clumps in the glass. Tastes like drywall | Taste and mixability are explicitly permitted copy (§1) |
| **BRAND X** | Blank white tub, no label, shifty | **Cannot produce a COA, a batch number, or a certificate.** Panics when asked | Dramatizes *proof as product* — the actual positioning |

**BRAND X is the strongest villain in the set.** It is the only one that argues the brand's
real differentiator, and the comedy writes itself: a supplement that runs out of the room
when someone asks to see the lab report.

## 4. ⚠️ The dramatic engine — the rule that governs every script

> **The conflict is never the product versus a symptom. It is always the product versus a
> rival, a habit, or an absence of proof.**

The original scripts fail as briefed. Concretely, from `approved-claims.md`:

| Beat in the original brief | Rule broken |
|---|---|
| "lasting energy and glowing skin" | §1 — "youthful glow / luminous skin" |
| Skin Crinkle drawing wrinkles | §1 — "reduces wrinkles / fine lines / signs of aging" |
| Collagen rebuilds the stomach walls | §1 — "Repairs / rebuilds / restores tissue" |
| Bloating evicted from the stomach | No authorised claim; condition-outcome framing |
| "sharp brains, high cheekbones" | Cognition + appearance outcomes |
| "You cause sugar crashes and breakouts" | Comparative claim + acne → medicinal (§6) |
| Matcha "lethal against body fatigue" | Rescuable *only* as an exact vitamin-C claim at threshold |

### What a compliant punchline looks like

The joke has to land on something you're allowed to say:

- ✅ **Taste and texture** — "it clumps", "tastes like drywall", "you cannot drink that"
- ✅ **Sugar content** — factual, on-label, a legitimate comparison
- ✅ **Proof** — COA, batch number, named origin, halal certificate with body + number
- ✅ **Ritual, heritage, culture** — Qahwa's whole character
- ✅ **Convenience** — mixes easily, neutral taste, one scoop
- ✅ **Being basic** — aesthetic judgement, not a health claim
- ❌ **Any bodily outcome** — skin, hair, nails, joints, energy, focus, digestion, mood

### The one exception, handled precisely

If a script wants a genuine benefit beat, it must attach to a **co-ingredient with an
authorised claim, at the source-of-nutrient threshold, in exact EFSA wording** — on screen
as text, quoted, not paraphrased into dialogue. E.g. an on-screen card reading:

> *Vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin.*

Confirm the formulation actually meets the threshold in product master data first. If it
doesn't, the claim isn't available and the beat is cut. Never put an authorised claim into
a character's mouth as a joke — quoting it verbatim on a card is the safe form.

### Before any script ships

Run `approved-claims.md` §7 (the decision tree) over **every line**, including throwaways
and on-screen text. Bias is hard toward refusal. Anything uncertain routes to `#approvals`
for a human read — per §8, the founder can override there, and the override is logged.

## 5. The three scenarios, recast

The original setups, rebuilt on the compliant engine. Punchlines land on proof, taste and
sugar rather than on symptoms.

### Scenario 1 — The Merger *(Coffee Collagen)*
Coffee is dragging the morning along at 5× speed and heading for a crash he announces
himself. Collagen arrives on a red carpet, they fuse into Coffee Collagen. **Resolution:**
the fused pack simply *is* both things — the ritual and the proof — while Basic Latte
watches, sweating, 30 grams of sugar deep. **Punchline** lands on Coffee reading his own
ingredient panel for the first time.

### Scenario 2 — The Audit *(Matcha Collagen)*
Replaces the stomach-lounge eviction entirely. **Brand X** is holding court, bragging.
Someone asks to see the certificate of analysis. Matcha arrives, silent, and simply
*produces the document*. **Resolution:** Brand X deflates — not a body, a blank white tub
with nothing to show. **Punchline:** Beige Scoop tries to help and clumps.

### Scenario 3 — The Coffee Shop Roast *(Qahwa Collagen)*
Runs nearly as briefed. Basic Latte brags about being trendy, insults Qahwa for being
traditional, Collagen steps out as the bodyguard. **Cut "breakouts" and "sharp brains and
high cheekbones."** The roast is about sugar, heritage and having nothing to declare.
Qahwa's closer stays exactly as written: *"You cannot rush perfection. Have some respect."*

## 6. Generation

Object-and-motion prompts only. Structure:

```
An animated 2D cartoon video. A sleek green matcha product pouch floats calmly in the
centre of frame. A brown splash of liquid coffee bursts in from the left, vibrating
rapidly. Fast-paced snap-cut action, vibrant colours, clean vector shapes,
no human figures, no faces.
```

- **Always end the prompt with `no human figures, no faces`.**
- Generate **9:16 natively** — do not reframe a 16:9 composition down; the empty
  top-and-bottom reads instantly as repurposed, which kills the UGC read.
- Never let a model render the pack's label copy — composite the real SVG from
  `packaging/`, or verify every glyph. Shipped packshots already carry generated typos
  ("arobica", "solvont-free").
- Save to `social/` beside the four existing reels.

### Voices — one source, pitch-shifted into the whole cast

**Decided 2026-08-31.** Lane B has **one voice**. The Feed voice (SAFAA's `Secondary` slot)
is the *source*, and every character is that same voice at a different pitch and rate.
Narration, when there is any, is the source at neutral.

So SAFAA still carries exactly two voices total — House and Feed — and the cartoon cast
costs nothing extra. Everything in the lane sounds like one show made by one person, which
is the register the format wants.

**The source voice:** comedic, raggedy, African American accent, hilarious, internet-native.

#### The pitch map — a starting point, not gospel

| Character | Pitch | Rate | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|
| **COLLAGEN** | +3 to +5 st | slightly slow | Smooth, glamorous, unbothered. Never rushes |
| **COFFEE** | +1 to +2 st | **1.5–2×** | Manic drill sergeant. The rate does most of the work here, not the pitch |
| **MATCHA** | −1 to −2 st | slow, quiet | Breathy, controlled, lands one sharp line then stops |
| **QAHWA** | −3 to −5 st | slow | Resonant, proud, unhurried |
| **Narration** | 0 | natural | The source voice as itself |

Two craft notes that decide whether this works:

- **Preserve formants when shifting.** A raw pitch shift without formant correction gives
  you chipmunk and demon, not characters. Any tool worth using exposes this separately.
- **Keep the total spread inside roughly 10 semitones.** Past that the voice stops sounding
  like one performer doing voices and starts sounding like a broken plugin.

#### The casting test — run this BEFORE locking any voice

The source has to survive being shifted in both directions. Generate the candidate saying
**all four signature lines at their four target pitches**, then listen for:

1. Does the comedy survive at −5 st? Raggedy timbre often flattens out when pitched down.
2. Does it survive at +5 st? Grit tends to turn thin and shrill.
3. **Does Qahwa still read as dignified?** This is the hard one. Qahwa is proud, royal,
   heritage — a comedic voice pitched down has to land *dramatic*, not *mocking*. If the
   register clash cannot be resolved by rate and delivery, Qahwa is the character that
   justifies an exception, and that exception needs a decision, not a workaround.
4. Can you tell all four apart with your eyes shut? If two collapse into each other, widen
   the rate difference before widening the pitch difference.

A source voice that fails on Qahwa but passes the other three is a *recast*, not a
compromise — the whole point of one source is that it carries the entire cast.

Pick the source with ElevenLabs Voice Design or Hume Octave (describe it, don't hunt a
library), then bring it in with `create_voice` so it lands as `voice_type: "element"`.
`voice_change` handles the per-character shifts.
