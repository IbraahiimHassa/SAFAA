# Supplier sample request — the three collagen drinks

For the CMO/blender already identified. Goal: **demo samples of Collagen Matcha, Qahwa +
Collagen and Collagen Coffee**, and — just as important — the paperwork that tells us whether
this supplier can actually hold the spec once we are paying for volume.

## Before you send it

1. **The brand name.** `TASKS.md` says the EUIPO/domain check comes before outreach names the
   brand. The email below is written so it works either way — replace `[BRAND]` with SAFA
   Nutrition if the check is clean, or delete the name and say "a Netherlands-based halal
   wellness brand launching Q1". Suppliers do not need the name to quote.
2. **Attach `docs/03-supplier-qc-specs.md`** — SKU 2 (the collagen) and SKU 4 (the blends).
   Sending the spec with the first email is the whole play: it changes what they send back,
   and it filters out the traders in one round instead of three.
3. **Offer to pay for samples and courier.** It costs ~€60–150 and it is the difference
   between a production-batch sample and a hand-made mock-up from the sales desk.
4. **Ship to a real NL address** you can receive at, and expect fish-derived powder to need
   a commercial invoice.

Dear [Name],

Thank you for your time. I am preparing the launch of [BRAND], a halal wellness brand based in

1. **Collagen Coffee** — single-origin arabica, ground for hot brewing, 414 g / 25 servings
2. **Collagen Matcha** — first-harvest ceremonial matcha, 286 g / 25 servings
3. **Qahwa + Collagen** — Arabic coffee with cardamom and saffron, 380 g / 25 servings

All three carry a **full 10 g dose of Type I marine collagen per serving** — not a token
inclusion — with vitamin C at 80 mg in the coffee and matcha, and MCT powder for body in the
coffee. No sugar, no sweeteners, no flavourings, no anti-caking agents, no maltodextrin in any
of the three.

I have attached our quality specification. I would rather send it now than after a first
production run, because it is the specification we will actually test against.

**What I would like to receive:**

- **Samples of your current standard versions** of each of the three (or the nearest equivalent
  you already produce), taken from a real production batch and batch-coded — not a lab mock-up.
  Two units of each if possible, so one can go to an independent lab.
- The **COA for the exact batch each sample comes from**, plus the raw-material COA for the
  collagen used in it.
- If a qahwa blend is not in your range, please say so directly — I would rather develop it with
  you as a custom formulation than receive an approximation.

We are happy to pay for the samples and the courier. Please send me an invoice.

**Documentation, so I can assess you properly:**

- Facility certificates: GMP, HACCP/ISO 22000, BRC or IFS if held — with expiry dates
- **Halal certificate** covering the blending and filling facility and the collagen line —
  certifying body, certificate number, scope and expiry. We publish the certifying body and
  number on our packaging, so "halal compliant" without a certificate is not usable to us.
- Marine collagen: **fish species, wild-caught or farmed, country of raw material and country
  of processing**, protein content, and the **molecular weight distribution report**
- Allergen statement, including cross-contact — in particular whether dairy protein runs on
  the same line
- Confirmation of whether you manufacture in your own plant or through a partner facility, and
  where that facility is

**Commercial questions:**

- MOQ per SKU, and the smallest **pilot batch** you will run
- Price per finished pouch at 500 / 1,000 / 2,500 units — filled, sealed and labelled to our
  artwork, and separately as bulk for us to co-pack
- Lead time for a pilot batch, and for repeat orders
- Whether you can develop to our formulation, or whether we would be adapting to your existing
  base blends
- For the qahwa blend: can you source true green cardamom pods, ground fresh, and negin-grade
  saffron with an ISO 3632 report per lot — or would you prefer we supply those materials?

**Two technical points worth raising early,** because they will shape what you send me:

- A 10 g collagen dose per serving is high for a flavoured blend. I am interested in how your
  base holds up at that loading — specifically mouthfeel and whether the blend stays homogeneous
  in the pouch rather than separating in transit.
- We will assess the collagen **hot**, prepared as the product is actually drunk, at around
  80 °C. Marine collagen that is clean in cold water can still show a marine note in a hot cup.
  If your deodorisation handles that, it is a genuine advantage and I would like to see it.

Once samples pass our tasting and an independent re-test, we would move to a small pilot batch,
then to volume on the usual terms — a share on order, the balance released against the batch
paperwork.

I look forward to your reply, and to the samples.

## When the samples arrive — the evaluation that decides it

Do this before any money moves. It takes an evening and a kettle.

**1. The hot cup.** Prepare each exactly as a customer would — 80 °C, the stated dose, the
stated volume. Smell it hot, taste it hot, then taste it again at 20 minutes when it has
cooled. You are listening for a marine note behind the coffee or matcha. This is the test
most brands skip and most customers fail the product on.

**2. The base drink alone.** Does the coffee taste like coffee you would drink without the
collagen? Is the matcha *green* — vivid, not olive? Is the cardamom fresh or dusty? Are the
saffron threads real threads? If the base is mediocre, no collagen spec saves it.

**3. Dissolution.** Stir ten seconds, no more. Look for sediment at the bottom of the glass
after five minutes, and for scum on the surface. Then leave the matcha pouch open on a
counter for a week and look at the colour again.

**4. The paperwork.** Read the COA against SKU 2 and SKU 4 in `docs/03`. The three numbers
that separate a real supplier from a broker: **protein ≥ 90%**, **molecular weight ≤ 3,000 Da
with an actual chromatogram**, and a **halal certificate with a named body, a number and an
expiry date**. Anything vague on those three is your answer.

**5. Independent re-test.** Send the second unit of the best-performing sample to an ISO 17025
lab for collagen assay and heavy metals. €200–400. Do it before the pilot batch, not after.

**6. Lock the golden sample.** Whichever sample you approve becomes the contractual reference —
"equal to golden sample" goes in the PO, and every future batch is judged against it. Keep it
sealed and dated.

## What this unblocks

Real COAs replace the placeholder assay values on the site and packaging (`README.md`
real-content manifest), real quotes replace the proposed €42/€44 price points, and the batch
pages get their first genuine numbers. Nothing on the storefront should carry a lab value that
did not come from one of these documents.
